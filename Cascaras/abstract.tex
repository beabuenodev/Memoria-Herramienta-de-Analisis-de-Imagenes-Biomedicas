\chapter*{Abstract}

\section*{\tituloPortadaVal}

This Bachelor's Thesis addresses the design and development of a computer vision-based tool for the segmentation of Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) images, with the aim of measuring the volumes of an organ and a lesion within a preclinical research framework.

In collaboration with ICTS BioImagen Complutense, a functional prototype has been developed to perform automatic segmentation for one of the use cases proposed by ICTS BioImagen Complutense: the segmentation of ischemic lesions in the mouse brain. Using two models, the tool performs the segmentation process on each slice, first on the whole brain and then on the damaged area. Then, using the segmentation data obtained for each region, the volumes of the whole brain and the damaged area are calculated.

Due to the scarcity of annotated data, which is common in medical environments, the methodology is based on the use of \textit{transfer learning} with a U-Net architecture. In addition, to provide robustness to the prototype and support the interpretation of its predictions, eXplainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques such as Grad-CAM and \textit{Integrated Gradients} are also applied.
